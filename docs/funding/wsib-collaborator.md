# WSIB research funding — as a funded collaborator

**Status:** ready to run. Two actions: one email to confirm timing, one researcher approach.

**Why this one.** WSIB bars private organizations from leading a project, but funds collaborators who supply data not otherwise available. That describes us precisely — and unlike most funding, it pays for exactly the thing we most need for every other conversation: evidence that our approach works.

**Why it is worth the effort even at a modest award size.** A peer-reviewed result with a named research institution behind it is worth more to the sector-council conversation, the provider conversation, and the eventual government conversation than the money is. We are buying credibility that cannot be bought any other way.

## Action 2 — approach a researcher to lead

We need an eligible principal applicant with a track record in work disability. The Institute for Work and Health is the natural first approach; McMaster's occupational health and rehabilitation researchers are the second.

See [`../outreach/contacts.md`](../outreach/contacts.md) for named researchers and verified public contact routes.

### The research question to propose

Bring a question, not a product. A researcher is not interested in our roadmap; they are interested in whether there is a publishable question with data behind it.

**Proposed:** *Can functional restriction data, recorded at the point of assessment and carried across service providers, identify workers at risk of prolonged work-disability duration earlier than current practice?*

Why this question and not another:

- Early identification of prolonged-duration risk is a standing priority in this field, and it is where our data shape is genuinely unusual.
- It needs longitudinal, cross-provider, individual-level restriction and outcome data — which is exactly what nobody has, because every existing record is trapped in a container the person has to stay inside.
- It is answerable with observational data, so it does not depend on our pilot completing first.
- It is not a question about our software. If the answer is no, that is still publishable, and we would want to know.

Confirm WSIB's current research priorities before finalizing the framing, and adjust the question to match their published language rather than ours.

### The approach email

> **Subject:** Cross-provider functional restriction data — possible research collaboration
>
> Dear Dr. [name],
>
> I am building a record that carries functional restrictions and employment outcomes with a person as they move between health and employment services in Ontario — deliberately not tied to a single employer, claim, or program, because our population is usually between all three.
>
> That produces a data shape I have not found elsewhere: individual-level restriction and outcome data, longitudinal, across multiple providers. I think it may support a question in your area — whether restriction data recorded at assessment can identify prolonged work-disability risk earlier than current practice.
>
> WSIB funds collaborators who supply data not otherwise available, but cannot fund a private organization as principal applicant. I am looking for a researcher who would find the question worth leading.
>
> I have no findings to defend and nothing to sell. If the question is uninteresting or already answered, I would value being told so.
>
> Would a short conversation be useful?
>
> [name], Trampoline

The last paragraph is not throat-clearing. A cold approach from a company to an academic reads as a request for endorsement unless it visibly invites a negative answer.

## What we can and cannot offer honestly

**Can offer:** the data structure and schema, our design rules and privacy posture, the software as research infrastructure, and access to the pilot cohort if the pre-apprenticeship pilot proceeds.

**Cannot offer, and must not imply:** existing data. We have none. Any proposal is prospective, and saying so up front is the only way this relationship survives contact with reality.
