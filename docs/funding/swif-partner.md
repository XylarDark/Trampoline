# Sectoral Workforce Innovation Fund — partner strategy

**Status:** ready to run. Not yet run.

**Why this one.** Best-timed opportunity in the whole funding scan: **continuous intake since 2026-08-25 until funds are exhausted**, up to **$10M over 36 months**, minimum **25% of requested ESDC funding from the private sector**, and digitization explicitly eligible. Continuous intake means no deadline forces a rushed Statement of Interest.

## Correction: we *can* lead. We probably still shouldn't.

The plan assumed for-profits could only participate, not lead. **That is wrong** — ESDC's eligible-applicant list names "for-profit organizations" outright.

The real constraints are different, and three of them bite us harder than the corporate form ever would:

1. **No single-company projects** without multi-employer sector benefit. A vendor pilot dressed as a sector project fails.
2. **No projects primarily benefiting one province** without national applicability. Our Hamilton-Niagara beachhead is precisely a single-province project, so a Trampoline-led application would need a national frame we cannot currently support.
3. **Minimum 100 participants for regional initiatives, 500 for national.** We have none.
4. Projects that "unnecessarily duplicate… **digital workforce platforms** without demonstrated value-added or transferability may not be prioritized." We would be assessed against the incumbents in [`../research/competitive-landscape.md`](../research/competitive-landscape.md), from a standing start.

So the sector-council route stays, but for an honest reason rather than a mistaken one: **we cannot meet the multi-employer, multi-province, and participant-volume tests on our own, and a council can.** The same ESDC page all but invites the arrangement, encouraging "workforce intermediaries, sector councils and convening organizations that can aggregate employer demand and coordinate workforce solutions."

## What runs in our favour

Two eligible-activity categories read almost like a description of the product:

- Objective 1 includes "certifications, **digital platforms**" and "**credential or training linked to jobs**."
- Objective 3 includes "development of modular, competency-based, and **portable credentials** aligned with employer and sector needs."

Also eligible: **wrap-around supports up to $2,000 per participant** for participants facing identified barriers, including accommodation, child care, and transportation — directly relevant to someone rebuilding health and income at once. One catch to raise with any lead before it appears in a budget: an organization providing wrap-around supports **must continue offering them after the federal funding ends**, as part of its sustainability plan. That is a permanent commitment bought with temporary money, and it is the lead's commitment, not ours.

Capital and IT spend is constrained: infrastructure is ineligible as a primary focus "unless tied to IT platforms to scale up online training across multiple provinces."

## Mechanics

Two steps. **Step 1 is a Statement of Interest** emailed to ESDC. Successful SOIs are invited to develop a full proposal. Responses are given **only to those selected**, and decisions are final with no appeal — so silence is the likely outcome of a weak SOI, and there is no feedback to learn from.

Verify the ceiling, cost share, and current parent-program name against canada.ca before use. Note three inconsistencies found in the public record: canada.ca gives the intake start as 2026-08-25 while the ministerial release dated 2026-08-28 says "starting today" and a third-party aggregator says 2026-09-09 (trust canada.ca; the intake is continuous regardless); the parent program is labelled "Sectoral Workforce **Development** Program" on two pages and "Sectoral Workforce **Solutions** Program" on a third; and trade press flagged the $10M and 75% figures as unconfirmed even though they appear on the official page.

## Essential context: the Workforce Alliances

SWIF prioritizes projects aligning with strategies proposed by the new **Workforce Alliances** — six of them, announced 2026-02-17, sharing $81M over five years, covering housing and construction, transportation and supply chains, advanced manufacturing, energy and electricity, mining and minerals, and the **care economy**. Each has a dedicated delivery organization plus a membership network intended to include equity-deserving communities.

**Those delivery organizations are effectively a pre-vetted list of credible SWIF leads.** Approaching an alliance lead is strictly better than approaching an unaffiliated association.

One frustration: the **Care Economy Alliance is the most relevant to our population and the least defined.** No delivery organization has been publicly named as of the sources checked — ESDC said leadership would be confirmed "in the coming weeks" in February 2026, and an April 2026 account still reported few details. Worth an enquiry precisely because it is unformed; an alliance still being assembled is easier to join than one already staffed.

Also treat the alliance funding total with care: reported as both $81M and $382.9M over five years, the larger figure from an unofficial source. Do not cite either to a funder.

## The position to argue

Not "fund our software." That fails, because the fund pays for sector workforce solutions rather than vendor products.

The argument is: **shared sector infrastructure that no single employer would build and every employer in the sector needs.**

Concretely, for a sector with a retention problem:

- Workers arrive with functional limitations that go unspecified until they fail on the floor, and 35.4% of employed Canadians with disabilities report an unmet accommodation need.
- Each employer solves it privately, badly, and repeatedly.
- The accommodation knowledge does not survive the worker changing employers, so the sector re-learns it every time.
- Clinician-to-workplace contact and accommodation offers are among the few interventions with *strong* evidence for shortening work disability.

That framing puts us in the position we want: a line in someone else's budget, solving a problem they have already published about.

## Who to approach

Ranked by federal workforce-funding track record, because a first-time applicant doubles the work and halves the odds.

### Tier 1 — documented federal precedent and a relevant mandate

**Food Processing Skills Canada.** The strongest single candidate. Delivered a **$9.7M** Sectoral Workforce Solutions Program project producing **over 23,000 micro-credentials** and training 2,100+ people, targeting up to 65% participation from equity-deserving groups. Executive Director **Jennefer Griffith** is described as a national leader in workforce development and **inclusive employment**, and has already built credential infrastructure closely analogous to ours — a Learning Recognition Framework, a certification platform, and a sector institute. She has solved our problem in her sector, which makes her either the ideal partner or the person best placed to explain why we are unnecessary. Either answer is worth the call. `jgriffith@fpsc-ctac.com`; general `info@fpsc-ctac.com`, 613-237-7988, Ottawa.

**Trucking HR Canada.** Leads the **Transportation and Supply Chain Workforce Alliance**, launched 2026-08-24, spanning road, rail, air, marine, and **warehousing**, with the stated aim of aligning training with real-time employer needs. Delivered a **$50.5M** SWSP project — the largest award on the list. President and CEO **Angela Splinter**. `theteam@truckinghr.com`, 613-244-4800, Ottawa.

**Canadian Council on Rehabilitation and Work.** Already on our interview list, and the precedent is the most on-point of any organization here: roughly **$3M through the National Workplace Accessibility Stream** for its AIM programme, which gives employers funding and expert advice for **workplace accommodations** and produced 150 workplace accommodation profiles. That is proposition 2 with federal money behind it. Also leads a federally funded project for federally regulated employers. `info@ccrw.org`, 416-260-3060, Toronto.

**BuildForce Canada.** Construction and skilled trades, industry-led since 2001, with a **$3.4M** SWSP project on workforce renewal and diversification. New Executive Director **Irwin Bess** started 2026-03-02 and is publicly committed to "modernizing digital tools" and "expanding partnerships" for an inclusive workforce — an unusually direct opening. `info@buildforce.ca`, 613-569-5552; communications `raiswell@buildforce.ca`.

**Excellence in Manufacturing Consortium.** Guelph, Ontario, so the closest to home. Co-leads the **Advanced Manufacturing Workforce Alliance**. Describes itself as a preferred federal partner, having delivered 6,500+ student work placements and over $38M to the sector since 2020, plus Ontario Skills Development Fund delivery. President **JP Giroux**.

**Ontario Disability Employment Network.** Best inclusive-hiring fit and Ontario-based, with a federally funded province-wide corporate training centre providing **sector-specific** employer training across healthcare, manufacturing, skilled trades, and hospitality. Co-operates the Ontario hub of a federally funded national mentorship programme. **One caution:** ODEN also runs a recruitment platform "created by and for people who have a disability," which is a potential competitive overlap as much as a partnership. Raise it directly rather than discovering it mid-proposal. No published partnerships email was verified — use the route on their site.

### Tier 2 — sector fit, no verified federal funding precedent

Retail Council of Canada (`info@retailcouncil.org`; Sebastian Prins, Director of Government Relations, `sprins@retailcouncil.org`). Supply Chain Canada Ontario Institute (Kim Sforza, `ksforza@supplychaincanada.com`). AdvantAge Ontario and the Ontario Long Term Care Association for long-term care — both reachable, neither with a federal workforce-funding track record found.

**Long-term care and personal support is a genuine gap in this list.** The better route for that sector is the Care Economy Workforce Alliance, which explicitly targets "workers and employers outside of formal systems, such as early childhood educators and **personal support workers**" — once it has a named lead.

### Qualifying questions before spending effort on any candidate

1. Do they have prior federal workforce funding? (Answered above for Tier 1.)
2. Can they name employer members who would participate, and reach 100 or 500 participants?
3. Can they supply a genuine multi-province frame, or would this be a single-province project?
4. Do they already have a digital workforce platform we would be duplicating?
5. Do they have the administrative capacity for a multi-year contribution agreement?
6. Are they willing to carry the wrap-around-supports sustainability commitment?

## The ask

> You have published on retention in [sector], and you have led federal workforce funding before. SWIF has continuous intake, pays for sector workforce solutions including digitization, and prioritizes projects aligned with the Workforce Alliances.
>
> We could apply on our own — for-profits are eligible — but we would fail the multi-employer and multi-province tests, and we would have no participants. You would not. So we would be a line in your budget, not a co-applicant competing for the lead.
>
> Would you be open to a conversation about whether accommodation specification and retention evidence belong in a Statement of Interest you lead?

Two phrases are doing the work. "A line in your budget, not a co-applicant" removes the question they will otherwise be asking, which is whether we are using them as a pass-through. And saying plainly that we *could* lead but would fail is more credible than implying we are barred — they can check the eligibility page in thirty seconds, and being caught overstating a constraint would end the conversation.

## What we supply to their Statement of Interest

Have this ready before the first call. A partner who has to extract material from us will not carry us.

| They need | We supply |
| --- | --- |
| The problem, with evidence | The retention-cliff and unmet-accommodation figures with sources, from [`../research/`](../research/) |
| The intervention, with an evidence base | Clinician-to-workplace contact and accommodation offers as strongly-evidenced levers |
| A measurement plan | A working derivation of the funder's own outcome definitions — not a schema, a running engine. Checkpoints at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months from job start, the hours and wage tests, per-document evidence acceptability, and a printable per-participant evidence pack. Read-only, under 107 tests, with every assumption we could not source named on the screen it affects. |
| A costed technology line | Build, hosting, and support, at a defensible day rate |
| Risk and privacy treatment | The eight design rules in [`../business-design.md`](../business-design.md) section 3, which pre-answer the privacy and human rights questions an assessor will raise |
| Cost-share evidence | The private cost share the fund requires. Confirm the percentage and what counts as eligible in-kind before promising anything. |

**The demo is the specific answer to the duplication exclusion.** The fund can deprioritize projects duplicating existing digital workforce platforms, and that is the exclusion most likely to catch us. Incumbents track placements. None of them tells a provider *why* a checkpoint is unclaimable, or which of those reasons a document can fix and which are structural. Show the caseload screen and the earned-but-not-provable count; it makes the distinction in less time than describing it takes.

## Honest position on our own credibility

We have no customers and no pilot yet. A sector council that puts us in a proposal is taking a real risk, and pretending otherwise wastes the relationship.

**A demo is not a pilot.** The twelve demo clients are synthetic fixtures engineered to produce specific verdicts, at `@example.invalid` addresses. They must never be described to a lead applicant as participants, as a cohort, or as data. Say what they are the moment the screen is shown; a partner who works it out later cannot tell "demo" from "fabricated," and would be right not to try.

Three things reduce the risk, and the first two come from work scheduled ahead of this:

1. The [retention interviews](../outreach/retention-interviews.md), which produce quotable provider demand.
2. Workforce planning board endorsement, which is cheap for them to give and materially strengthens someone else's application.
3. The read-only demo — twelve synthetic cases across ten distinct verdicts, running on a laptop with no Docker and no deployment. It takes the delivery-capability question off the table in ninety seconds.

Sequence this **after** the first two. The demo is a real asset now, but it does not substitute for either of them, and it is important to be clear about why: a council's risk is not "can they build it," which the demo answers, but "will providers use it," which only the interviews answer. Lead with provider quotes and show the demo second.
