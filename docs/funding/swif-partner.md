# Sectoral Workforce Innovation Fund — recruiting a lead applicant

**Status:** ready to run. Not yet run.

**Why this one.** Best-timed opportunity in the whole funding scan: continuous intake rather than a fixed deadline, a large ceiling, and digitization costs explicitly eligible. Continuous intake also means there is no deadline forcing a rushed, weak Statement of Interest — the constraint is finding the right lead, not the calendar.

**Why we cannot lead.** For-profits qualify as participants but cannot represent a single enterprise, and the fund is aimed at organizations that aggregate employer demand and coordinate workforce solutions. That is a sector council, an industry association, or an employer coalition. It is not us.

Verify the fund's current name, ceiling, cost-share, and eligibility against the primary source before approaching anyone. See [`../research/funding-landscape.md`](../research/funding-landscape.md) for what the research found, and treat every figure as needing re-confirmation.

## The position to argue

Not "fund our software." That fails, because the fund pays for sector workforce solutions rather than vendor products.

The argument is: **shared sector infrastructure that no single employer would build and every employer in the sector needs.**

Concretely, for a sector with a retention problem:

- Workers arrive with functional limitations that go unspecified until they fail on the floor, and 35.4% of employed Canadians with disabilities report an unmet accommodation need.
- Each employer solves it privately, badly, and repeatedly.
- The accommodation knowledge does not survive the worker changing employers, so the sector re-learns it every time.
- Clinician-to-workplace contact and accommodation offers are among the few interventions with *strong* evidence for shortening work disability.

That framing puts us in the position we want: a line in someone else's budget, solving a problem they have already published about.

## Who to approach

Target sectors, chosen because they hire our population at volume and have documented retention problems: long-term care and personal support, warehousing and supply chain, construction and skilled trades, food processing, and retail.

See [`../outreach/contacts.md`](../outreach/contacts.md) for candidate organizations and verified contact routes.

Qualifying questions before spending effort on any candidate:

1. Have they led or partnered on federal workforce funding before? A first-time applicant doubles the work and halves the odds.
2. Do they have a published position on retention, labour shortage, or inclusive hiring we can attach to?
3. Can they name employer members who would participate? The fund wants aggregated employer demand, and a council without engaged members cannot supply it.
4. Do they have the administrative capacity to hold a multi-year contribution agreement?

## The ask

> You have published on retention in [sector]. There is a federal fund with continuous intake that pays for sector workforce solutions, including digitization, and it is looking for organizations that aggregate employer demand — which is you, not us.
>
> We would be a line in your budget, not a co-applicant competing for the lead. Would you be open to a conversation about whether accommodation specification and retention evidence belong in a Statement of Interest you lead?

The phrase "a line in your budget, not a co-applicant" is the important one. It removes the question they will otherwise be asking, which is whether we are trying to use them as a pass-through.

## What we supply to their Statement of Interest

Have this ready before the first call. A partner who has to extract material from us will not carry us.

| They need | We supply |
| --- | --- |
| The problem, with evidence | The retention-cliff and unmet-accommodation figures with sources, from [`../research/`](../research/) |
| The intervention, with an evidence base | Clinician-to-workplace contact and accommodation offers as strongly-evidenced levers |
| A measurement plan | Our outcome-evidence schema: referrals with acceptance, placements, employment spells with hours and wage, milestones, follow-ups at the reported months |
| A costed technology line | Build, hosting, and support, at a defensible day rate |
| Risk and privacy treatment | The eight design rules in [`../business-design.md`](../business-design.md) section 3, which pre-answer the privacy and human rights questions an assessor will raise |
| Cost-share evidence | The private cost share the fund requires. Confirm the percentage and what counts as eligible in-kind before promising anything. |

## Honest position on our own credibility

We have no customers and no pilot yet. A sector council that puts us in a proposal is taking a real risk, and pretending otherwise wastes the relationship.

Two things reduce that risk, and both come from work already scheduled ahead of this:

1. The [retention interviews](../outreach/retention-interviews.md), which produce quotable provider demand.
2. Workforce planning board endorsement, which is cheap for them to give and materially strengthens someone else's application.

Sequence this **after** both. Approaching a sector council with nothing but a repository is the fastest way to be politely declined by the one lead applicant we most needed.
